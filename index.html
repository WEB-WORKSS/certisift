The problem

Certificates arrive faster than QA can read them.

Every incoming lot depends on a document someone has to read carefully. Under time pressure, that review is the first thing to slip.

Today · manual review

  1. Every incoming lot ships with a Certificate of Analysis (CoA) that QA must compare, line by line, against an internal specification.
  2. Formats differ by supplier and lab: parameter names, units, methods and layouts all vary.
  3. Reviews are manual and inconsistent under time pressure, so near-limit results, missing tests and copied values slip through.
  4. A missed deviation can mean a production hold, scrapped batches, a customer complaint or a recall.
  5. Supplier performance lives in spreadsheets, so recurring problems are noticed late.

With CertiSift

  1. The certificate is parsed the moment it arrives: supplier, lot, dates and every result with its unit.
  2. Different parameter names and number formats are normalised to your specification.
  3. Each result is checked against your limits, including near-limit and missing tests.
  4. Results are compared with the supplier's history to catch outliers and copied values.
  5. The reviewer records a decision, and supplier performance updates automatically.

AI, used carefully

The rules decide. The AI reads and explains.

Pass, warn and fail come from your specification and deterministic checks you can trace. AI is used to read difficult documents and explain findings, and your reviewer makes every release decision.

What the AI does

  • Reads scanned PDFs and images (planned, with Amazon Textract)
  • Interprets unfamiliar certificate layouts and maps parameter names (planned, with Amazon Bedrock)
  • Explains why a lot was flagged and lists the checks behind it
  • Suggests a note for the reviewer to edit

What it never does

  • Release, hold or reject a lot
  • Change your specification limits
  • Fill in a missing test result
  • Use one customer's certificates to answer another's questions

How We Use Amazon Web Services

CertiSift's production platform is designed as a serverless workflow on AWS: certificates go into encrypted storage, Amazon Textract and Amazon Bedrock turn scanned and unfamiliar layouts into structured results, and AWS Lambda checks them against your specification. Today the verification engine runs in the browser in the live demo.

    Amazon S3AWS KMSAmazon DynamoDB

    AWS Step FunctionsAWS Lambda

These services are proposed, not yet deployed. The live demo runs in your browser on sample data. The full architecture →

  • Amazon Textract: Extract text and tables from scanned and PDF certificates.
  • Amazon Bedrock: Interpret unfamiliar layouts, normalise parameter names, assist anomaly review.
  • AWS Lambda: Run extraction, matching and report generation steps.
  • AWS Step Functions: Orchestrate the upload → extract → interpret → match → report workflow.
  • Amazon S3: Encrypted storage of uploaded certificates and generated reports.
  • Amazon DynamoDB: Supplier records, specifications and verification results.
  • Amazon API Gateway: Secure API for the web app and future integrations.
  • Amazon Cognito: User sign-in and per-customer tenant isolation.
  • AWS KMS: Customer-managed encryption keys for documents and records.
  • Amazon CloudWatch: Logs, metrics and alerting.

Is CertiSift available today?

CertiSift is building its initial product. The live demo runs the verification engine in your browser on text-based certificates and fictional suppliers. Early-access requests are open for pilots with food and beverage QA teams.

Which documents can it read?

Today: certificates as plain text or CSV, pasted or uploaded. Scanned PDFs and images will be handled through Amazon Textract in the production platform.

What does the AI do?

In the planned platform, Amazon Textract reads scanned certificates and Amazon Bedrock interprets unfamiliar layouts and explains findings. Pass, warn and fail come from deterministic checks against your specification, not from the AI.

Does it replace our QA team's judgement?

No. CertiSift does the line-by-line comparison and highlights what needs attention. The release decision, and the note explaining it, stay with your reviewer.

Who can change what?

QA managers own specifications, reviewers record release decisions, and auditors get read-only access. In the demo roles are not enforced; in production they are enforced on the server.

Can we use our own specifications?

Yes. Specifications are set up per material with ranges, minimums, maximums, pathogen requirements and the parameter names your suppliers use.

How much does it cost?

Proposed launch pricing starts at $149 a month for one site. Final pricing is set with pilot customers, and no payment is taken today. See the Pricing page.

Where is our data stored?

In the demo, everything stays in your own browser's local storage. The production platform is designed for encrypted storage on AWS, with customer data separated by tenant.
